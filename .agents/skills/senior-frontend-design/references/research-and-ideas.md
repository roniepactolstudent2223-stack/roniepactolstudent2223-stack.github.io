# Research and concept development

Use for a new identity, a substantial redesign, or an explicit request for alternatives. A spacing fix or an established reference does not need a full research round.

## Find references outside the obvious category

Start with a bounded search across different questions. For a full page, four to six useful examples across several independent sources is often sufficient; a header may need only one or two. Stop when the references support a coherent decision. Search more only to resolve an actual gap.

Choose complementary evidence:

- A reference in the subject's own world: a process, tool, publication, physical object, or environment.
- A website showing relevant hierarchy or navigation in use.
- Typography, photography, illustration, or print design that offers a different visual vocabulary.
- An interaction example only if interaction is part of the brief.

Follow gallery credits to the original designer or live implementation where possible. Include less familiar studios, regions, or historical periods when relevant to the subject, without reducing cultures to decorative motifs. Popularity is neither proof of quality nor a reason to reject a useful reference.

Search by a concrete design problem. Examples to adapt, not stock queries for every project:

- `architect studio portfolio project index mobile navigation`
- `type specimens technical manuals diagram hierarchy`
- `museum collection conservation labels object photography`
- `independent music venue printed programme identity`
- `developer portfolio interactive code demonstration accessible`

Prefer primary descriptions and direct observation. A tutorial explains a technique; it does not establish that the technique fits the current audience. A screenshot shows one size and state; it does not establish responsiveness.

## Turn observations into a new composition

Use a compact record when helpful:

| Reference | Observed principle | Proposed adaptation | Leave behind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specific URL and creator | What the example actually does | How it supports this audience and content | Branding, assets, or behavior that does not fit |

Combine principles from different contexts: a field guide's annotations could inform project explanations; a contact sheet's sequencing could shape a work index. Do not transplant a reference's whole layout, colors, type, wording, and interaction as a bundle unless the user explicitly requested a faithful reproduction and provided appropriate materials.

Collecting a reference is not permission to reuse its assets. Verify the relevant license or use the user's own material. Do not hotlink another site's media as a default asset strategy.

## Generate directions through meaningful differences

Use the subject's content to explore different relationships. These questions produce more variety than a list of fashionable styles:

- Should users begin with the work itself, a process demonstration, a narrative, or an index?
- Is the useful rhythm a continuous reading sequence, a browsable collection, a comparison, or an interactive explanation?
- What deserves the strongest contrast: an artifact, a finding, a portrait, or a piece of functionality?
- Would imagery behave as evidence, atmosphere, instruction, or navigation?
- What does touch change about this idea?

For a developer portfolio, hypothetical directions might include a project index with immediately inspectable implementation details, a narrative around one difficult engineering problem, or a small interactive instrument showing an actual capability. These are examples of content strategies, not templates to repeat or instructions to invent achievements.

For each candidate, identify the audience benefit, memorable idea, responsive transformation, asset needs, and implementation cost. Reject directions that depend on nonexistent content, obscure the primary task, or require unjustified complexity. Prefer the strongest fit over the most novel effect.

## Test the recommendation

Ask whether the idea can be understood before interaction, works without hover, and still has character when motion is reduced. Identify the closest familiar template and explain how the content or structure differs. If the only distinction is color, revise the concept.

Novelty should be supported by specific decisions and sources. Do not claim that a design is unused, unprecedented, or guaranteed to avoid all resemblance to existing work.
